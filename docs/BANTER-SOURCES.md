# Computer banter: sources and spirit

The words are in `api/_voices.js` (shared by the phones and the online server). Spoken quips only, no song lyrics.
"Real" means the Beatles (or Bowie, Marley) said it, nearly word for word. "Adapted" means a real quip turned to fit the
card table. Everything else is written in their spirit and is not a quotation; the app never claims otherwise.

## Real (press conferences 1963-65, A Hard Day's Night, Royal Variety, the rooftop concert)
- John: "Turn left at Greenland" (how did you find America); "Why not!" (why the name Beatles); "You're joking!" (formal
  training); "Do me a favour!" and "The end, daddy-o" and "Fruitcake" (journalists), "Harmless" (fans) from his handwritten
  answers to a teen magazine; "I haven't got a nice personality"; "It must be the weather"; "Count the money";
  "He ought to see a doctor about that" (a critic); "...and flattened" (George: "we feel flattered"); "His dad was a Mars bar"
  (Paul); "Just Paul. Just our Paul."; "George's dressing gown is definitely a big attraction"; "Rattle your jewellery"
  (Royal Variety, 1963); "I hope we've passed the audition" (rooftop, 1969).
- Paul: "Oh aye! That's right."; "I just sort of run" (how do you avoid being recognised); "No comment!"; "Probably England"
  (favourite foreign country); "Well, I haven't seen myself do it" (sleeping with eyes open); "Stamp Out Detroit";
  "Thank you, John." (after John called him a good influence).
- George: "Arthur" (what do you call that hairstyle, also in the film); "Sail on me yacht"; "We're not" (not very good
  musicians); "Oh. That's nice." (millionaires); "Usually we have to cut it into four" (an award each); "I don't like your tie"
  (to George Martin); "No screams"; "Individually we're all crummy musicians"; "Well, we feel flattered."
- Ringo: "I'm a mocker" (mod or rocker, film); "Hair-don't"; "Any minute now" (retiring); "What are you doing tonight?"
  (do you get many dates); "We just come for the money"; "Does he buy our records?"; "To be anything, you're supposed to
  practise a couple of hours a day"; "Have you ever tried talking to two hundred people at once?"; and his own coinages
  "a hard day's night", "tomorrow never knows" and "eight days a week"; "I've got blisters on my fingers!" (end of Helter
  Skelter). "Peace and love" is his sign-off.
- Bowie: "I promise it won't be boring"; "Tomorrow belongs to those who can hear it coming"; Ziggy's "instant star, just
  add water and stir". Marley: "Money is numbers and numbers never end."

## Adapted
"He ought to see a doctor about that" and "Flattered? You're flattened" aimed at the player; "Eyes open, Paul?" (George);
"Thank you, John" turned sarcastic when Paul answers John; Ringo's "Hair-don't" and George's "Arthur" thrown at each other.

## In their spirit (written for Switch, not quotes)
The rest: gloats, sulks, last-card teases, digs at "user" and "any", and the rest of the digs between computers.
Beatles-inspired fictional banter. Before a store launch, check permissions for using their names and likenesses.

## Card talk (what they say about the cards)
`api/_cardTalk.js`. Song titles are used as puns, never lyrics: "Taxman" (George, black king), "Hello, goodbye" and
"Ob-la-di, ob-la-da" and "Yesterday" (Paul), "Eight days a week", "Don't pass me by" and "A hard day's night" (Ringo), "Come
together", plus Bowie's "Ch-ch-changes", "Under pressure", "Ashes to ashes", "Fame", "Life on Mars?" and "Heroes",
Ziggy's "Five Years", "Moonage Daydream" and "Rock 'n' Roll Suicide", and Marley's "Exodus", "Three Little Birds",
"Stir It Up", "Get Up, Stand Up", "Redemption Song" and "I Shot the Sheriff". Real quips reused where a card fits them
(John's "Turn left at Greenland" on a jack that reverses play, "Count the money" on a win, "Rattle your jewellery" on an ace).
Everything else is written in their spirit.
