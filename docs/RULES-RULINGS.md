# Switch rules: rulings (sacrosanct)

These are the owner's decisions on every point the written rules left open. The app's engine, the in-app rules text and the tests must all agree with this list. Change it only on the owner's say-so.

Decided 7 Nov 2026, after playing 10,000 simulated games (2 to 6 players, four different playing styles) straight from the written rules.

| # | Question | Ruling |
|---|----------|--------|
| 1 | Can an 8 be the winning card with two players? | Yes. An 8 can win. Only a 7 (always), a jack (two players) and two or four jacks played together (any table, ruling 18) cannot win. They may still be played as the last cards, but they force a pickup (ruling 19). |
| 2 | Can a 2 or a king be the winning card? | Yes. You win at once and nobody picks up. The same goes for an ace. |
| 3 | Must the first card of a same-number set match the suit? | No. The first card must match the suit or the number of the previous card. The last card played decides the suit. |
| 4 | Does picking up end your turn? | Yes, for both the single card on a normal turn and for penalties from twos or kings. Only a 7 lets you play again. |
| 5 | Several 8s in one play | Still one missed turn, however many 8s. |
| 6 | Several 7s in one play | Still one extra turn, however many 7s. |
| 7 | A black and a red king in one play | Allowed. How they count is set by ruling 14: card by card, in the order played. |
| 8 | After a 7, if you cannot or will not play | Pick up one card and your turn ends. |
| 9 | Order of cards when the deck runs out | Turn the played pile (not the top card) face down without shuffling, so the oldest card is drawn first. |

## How these are enforced

- `src/switch/rules.ts` is the engine. `scripts/table-sim.mjs` is a second implementation written only from the rules text, for 2 to 6 players.
- `tests/table-sim.test.mjs`: 300 full games per table size must finish, keep all 52 cards, and never win on a forbidden card.
- `tests/switch-rules-sim.spec.ts`: the engine must agree with the simulator on every legal move and every result, for 2, 3 and 4 players.
- `scripts/contentious.mjs` measures how often each open point comes up. `npm run test:rules` runs the quick checks.
- The in-app rules (`src/content.ts`) say all of the above in plain words.

## Scoring rulings (owner decisions, 7 Nov 2026)

10. **Darts:** the first player to three legs wins the set (3-0, 3-1 and 3-2 all count). No two-clear-legs rule. First to the agreed number of sets (3 or 5) wins the match and the scorekeeper then stops.
11. **Knock Out (owner revised 2026-11-07):** each round, the player who finishes last loses and is out; everyone else progresses. Six players become 5, then 4, 3, 2, and the last two play for the win. Number of cards held is irrelevant.
12. **Group Play / leagues:** finishing places earn points (number of players for 1st down to 1). Level on points: most round wins; still level: a deciding game. The app never breaks a tie silently.
13. **Scorekeeper:** follows the written rules. Domino and one v one stop at the target and show the winner; domino records whether an ace counts as 1 or 11; darts offers 3 or 5 sets.

`scripts/scoring-sim.mjs` plays every format through the real scoring modules.

14. **Kings count card by card, in the order played (8 Nov 2026, replaces the net-count part of ruling 7; revised 15 Nov 2026: a red king never passes a penalty on).** A black king adds 5, a red king takes 5 off, never below nothing. **If the top card laid is a red king, the player who laid it picks up whatever is left (their turn then ends) and nothing passes on; only a black king on top passes the pile-up to the next player.** E.g. facing 10, one red king leaves you picking up 5 yourself; facing 5, one red king means you pick up nothing. One black = 5; black on 5 = 10; one red on 10 = 5; two reds on 10 = 0; black then red = 0; red then black = 5; two reds then two blacks = 10; one red then two blacks = 10. A lone red king with nothing pending is an ordinary card. Mixed sets may still be played; the order laid down decides the result.

15. **One v One is played to a number of games (7 Nov 2026, owner request; replaces the odd best-of legs and the points target).** The host types how many games to play, any whole number: with 4, the first player to win 4 games takes the match and the scorekeeper stops. Scores count games won. The scorekeeper also offers best of an odd number of games (first to a majority wins), added at the owner's request on the same day (commit 1cd9239).

16. **Leagues can be different sizes, and the draw is random (7 Nov 2026, owner request).** Each league has 3 to 6 players (for example two leagues of 4 and one of 3), and plays as many games as it has players: 3 games for 3 players, 4 for 4. Points are the number of players in that league for first, down to 1 for last. Names are typed first and the app draws who goes in which league at random. Playoffs: the top two in each league contest the championship; everyone else (third place and below) contests the plate, paired across leagues where possible, and with an odd number one plate player gets a bye. The Switch Cup is drawn at random too, and with an odd number a random player gets a bye each round.

17. **League playoffs are knockouts (8 Nov 2026, owner request).** The championship is a knockout among the top two from every league (two leagues: 4 players, 3 games: 4 become 3, 3 become 2, the last two play for the title). The plate is the same knockout among everyone ranked third or lower. Each game, the player who finishes last drops out. One player alone in the plate takes a bye.

18. **Two or four jacks mean play again (13 Nov 2026, owner ruling).** One or three jacks reverse the direction of play; with two players that brings play straight back, so the same player plays again. Two or four jacks never reverse: the direction stays as it was and whoever played them plays again, at every table size. Because they mean play again, two or four jacks cannot be a player's last cards (the same reason a seven cannot). One or three jacks can still be the winning play with three or more players. This replaces the earlier reading that two jacks cancel out and play moves on.

19. **A seven or jacks that cannot win may still be played as the last cards, but they force a pickup (14 Nov 2026, owner clarification of the finishing rule and rulings 1 and 18).** A seven (any table), a jack (two players) and two or four jacks together (any table) cannot win the game. They are NOT illegal as the last cards: the player puts them down, picks up one card from the deck, says "Last Card" again, and their turn ends. They are left holding that one card and the next player plays. Direction of play is unchanged, nothing else happens (no extra turn), and the played cards still set the suit. This replaces the earlier engine behaviour that refused the play altogether.

20. **Last Card is the player's own call, and finishing needs it too (14 Nov 2026, owner request).** Against the computer there is an always-visible Last Card button at the side of the table; nothing ever prompts for it. Playing out your final cards (a single card, a set such as two tens, or a seven or jacks that cannot win) without having called it means you do not win on that play: the play stands and you pick up two cards. Holding exactly one card without having called it lets a computer spot it and you pick up two. A call is used up by the play it covers, so after a pickup you call again. Engine: `penaliseMissedLastCard` in `src/switch/table.ts`; screen: `src/screens/TableGame.tsx`.

## Computer game types (owner decisions, 14 Nov 2026)

21. **Replaced by ruling 38 (18 Nov 2026): Switch is only ever played with one deck.** *Was:* **Tables of 7 and 8 use two decks (owner choice, 14 Nov 2026).** One deck cannot deal seven cards each to 7 or 8 players (8 x 7 = 56). Tables of 7 or 8 shuffle two 52-card decks together (104 cards). Dealing, play, penalties, jacks and every other rule are unchanged. A table is you plus 1 to 7 computers, so 2 to 8 seats. A card from the second deck is the same card to the rules (it matches suit and number exactly as its twin does); only the screen and the engine tell the two copies apart.

22. **Computer game types (owner request, 14 Nov 2026).** PLAY offers: Single game, Knock Out, Group Play (two leagues of 4 at a full table of 8), Switch Cup, Darts scoring and Dominoes scoring. Every game of every type is played as real Switch at the table, and every type is recorded in the player's stats, trophies and badges. The computer players are John, Paul, George, Ringo, Ziggy, Marley and Bowie, in that order. (Ruling 38: you face up to five of them at once.)
    - **Knock Out:** ruling 25 (1 v 1 games, winners go through).
    - **Group Play:** ruling 12 and 16 (place points, round wins, a deciding game), ruling 17 and 28 (knockout play-offs, played on to the last card-holder).
    - **Switch Cup:** head-to-head knockout rounds as in the scorekeeper; games between two computers are simulated and shown as a result.
    - **Darts scoring:** each Switch game is a leg; first to three legs wins a set (ruling 10); the player picks 3 or 5 sets to win the match.
    - **Dominoes scoring (reverse of the scorekeeper's domino):** the player picks a number. After every game each player adds up the cards still in their hand (ace 1, 2 to 10 face value, jack 11, queen 12, king 13; the winner of the game has none, so adds nothing) and adds that to their running total. The first player to reach or pass the chosen number LOSES the match. If two or more pass it in the same game, the highest total loses. The player with the lowest total wins. Totals are kept for every player.

24. **A league round is played on until every place is decided (owner correction, 14 Nov 2026; clarifies ruling 12 and 22).** A league game does not stop when the first player is out. With four players: the first out scores 4 points and leaves the table, the other three keep playing, the next out scores 3, and the last two play on for 2 and 1. With three players it is 3, 2 and 1; every league size follows the same pattern (size for first down to 1 for last). The rest of the table plays by the same rules on the cards that are left: a jack finishing or bouncing play back follows the number of players still in, so with two left it is a two-player game. A deciding game between level players is played the same way. Going out without having called Last Card (ruling 20) puts the player back in with two cards and no place. Engine: `playOn` games in `src/switch/table.ts`; league games and deciding games in `src/switch/series.ts`; `tests/switch-play-on.spec.ts`. Knock Out is separate (ruling 11) and the play-offs (ruling 17) are unchanged.

25. **Knock Out against the computer is 1 v 1 (owner correction, 14 Nov 2026; replaces ruling 11 for the PLAY tab).** Players are drawn in pairs and each pair plays one game of Switch, head to head. The winner goes through to the next round, the loser is out, and the pattern repeats until one player is left. With an odd number one randomly drawn player gets a bye each round. Games between two computers are simulated. This is the same bracket as the Switch Cup. The scorekeeper's Knock Out (ruling 11) and the guide text are unchanged until the owner says otherwise.

26. **Last Card goes in before the final card is selected (owner clarification, 14 Nov 2026; adds to ruling 20).** To win with a set of the same number (two, three or four cards), the player selects the first card or cards, taps Last Card, then selects the final card. Saying it after the final card is already selected is too late, and the player picks up two cards as in ruling 20. Calling before selecting anything also counts. With a single card left, that card is both first and final, so calling after selecting it but before pressing Play also counts (owner decision, 15 Nov 2026, after a player reported a penalty). The Last Card button sits in the player's own card area. Screen: `src/screens/TableGame.tsx`; tests: `tests/switch-2-table.spec.ts`.

27. **Pace at the table (owner request, 14 Nov 2026; refined by ruling 29: a move with only one legal action is quick).** A computer takes at least 5 seconds over every move that has a choice in it, at every pace setting (the pace setting still changes how fast cards fly). You have 5 seconds to say Last Card: a computer cannot spot that you forgot until 5 seconds of its turn have passed, and those 5 seconds count as its thinking time. Games between computers are still instant. "Play selected" always stays inside the phone, riding just above the tab bar. Automated test browsers skip the waits unless a test sets `switchRealPace` to 1. Code: `MIN_THINK_MS` and `LAST_CARD_GRACE_MS` in `src/switch/presentation.ts`.

29. **Computer difficulty, tactics and personalities (owner decisions, 14 Nov 2026).** The three levels are called Easy, Medium and Hard (stored as relaxed, standard and sharp). Easy plays with no tactics, Medium with three (tempo, shield, aces), Hard with all seven (adds patience, hunter, counting, planner). Tactics only choose between moves the rules already allow; none changes a rule. The seven tactics: *tempo* plays 7s (and heads up 8s and jacks) one at a time because several count once (rulings 5, 6, 18); *shield* does not throw both 2s or both black kings together and keeps one red king back; *aces* holds aces, the easiest card to go out on, and spends one only when nearly out; *patience* picks up a card instead of spending an ace or black king when nobody is close to going out; *hunter* saves attacks for the player nearest going out; *counting* works out from the played pile the odds the next player holds a 2 or king; *planner* plans the whole run of cards after a 7. They use only what a person at the table can see (own hand, played pile, card counts). Each computer has a personality that sets how strongly it leans on each tactic: John the Closer, Paul the Playmaker, George the Counter, Ringo the Rock, Ziggy the Ace Collector, Marley the Patient One, Bowie the Chameleon. A level only allows its own tactics, so a personality colours them but cannot add one. **When a computer plays for an absent human (ruling 23) it uses no tactics.** A computer with exactly one legal move (such as having to pick up) makes it quickly (`FORCED_THINK_MS`); only moves with a choice keep the 5 second thinking time of ruling 27. Code: `src/switch/computerTactics.ts`, `tableAi.ts`, `ai.ts`, `api/_room.js`; measured by `scripts/ai-selfplay.mjs`; tests: `tests/switch-tactics.spec.ts`. Retune (15 Nov 2026): Hard also keeps its hand connected when two players are left (`handCohesion` in `computerTactics.ts`; measured worse with three or more, so heads up only). Measured with `node scripts/ai-levels.mjs 3000`: Medium beats Easy about 75%, Hard beats Medium about 56% heads up, and at a 4-seat table Hard takes 27% against a 25% fair share. Heads-up Switch is largely luck, so a Hard edge over Medium of more than a few points needs search, not tactics.

## Online play (owner decision, 14 Nov 2026)

30. **Ways to play online (owner decisions, 14 Nov 2026).** An online table offers every way to play: a single game, Knock Out, Switch Cup, Group Play, Darts scoring and Dominoes scoring, with the same rules as against the computer except one: **Knock Out with friends is the game-night version (ruling 11), not 1 v 1 rounds (ruling 25).** Everyone still in plays each game together and it is played on (ruling 24) until one player is left holding cards; that player is out. Three left play the same way, and the last two play a 1 v 1 final. Games are played side by side, as at a real card night (ruling 32). Anyone not in a game being played (knocked out, or between games) can watch any game with every hand face down and can still send reactions. Group Play needs at least 6 seats and Knock Out at least 3. Every game a person plays is a stat, and how the whole tournament ended is saved too. Code: `api/_room.js`, `src/switch/series.ts` (`knockout: "last-out"`), `src/screens/OnlineTable.tsx`; tests: `tests/room.test.mjs`, `tests/switch-online-play.spec.ts`.

23. **Turn clock at an online table.** A player who has made no move 30 seconds into their turn is told the computer will play their move in 10 seconds, with a visible countdown to them and to the table. At 40 seconds the computer plays that one move for them. A move made first always wins. The computer calls Last Card correctly when it plays for someone, so a computer-played move never costs a human the two-card penalty (ruling 20). No rule of play changes: the engine is the same. Open: what happens after repeated computer-played moves (see the online play spec). **Last Card online:** a player holding one card without having said it is spotted 5 seconds after their move reached the table, plus one second for the call to travel; another phone checking in cannot cut that short.

28. **Group Play play-offs are knock-outs played on to the last card-holder (owner request, 14 Nov 2026; sharpens ruling 17).** Once the leagues (and any deciding games) are played out, the next round is the knock-outs. With four players in the championship, all four play one game together and it is played on, as a league round is (ruling 24): players go out as they empty their hands and the rest carry on until only one player is left holding cards. That player is out. The remaining three play the same way and the last one holding cards is out, then the last two play a 1 v 1 final, won by the first to go out. The plate is the same knock-out among everyone ranked third or lower. Engine: `koPending` in `src/switch/series.ts` (play-on whenever more than two are left); test: `tests/switch-play-on.spec.ts`.

32. **Big online nights: up to 16, games at the same time (owner decisions, 14 Nov 2026; table size changed by ruling 38: read every 8 below as 6, and Group Play as up to 12).** An online table holds up to 16 people and computers for Knock Out, Switch Cup and Group Play; a single game, Darts and Dominoes stay at 8 because everyone plays in one game. A tournament's games run at the same time, never one after another: every Cup match of a round at once, both leagues side by side, and a Knock Out of more than 8 split as evenly as possible onto the fewest tables of up to 8 (16 makes two tables of 8, 12 two of 6). At each Knock Out table the game is played on until one player is left holding cards, and that player is out; once every table has finished, those still in are drawn onto tables again, until 8 or fewer play at one table down to the 1 v 1 final. Group Play keeps two leagues, however many play (12 make two leagues of 6), and its play-offs split the same way when more than 8 are in one. Games with only computers in them are played in real time at the computers' pace, so their results come in alongside everyone else's. Each game is dealt 20 seconds after the game that leads to it ends (the host can deal waiting games straight away), so one slow table never holds up another. Every finished game, every player knocked out and the winner are announced to everyone at the table, wherever they are in the app. Code: `api/_room.js` (`room.tables`, `nextEvent`, `news`), `koStart`/`koApply` in `src/switch/series.ts`; tests: `tests/room.test.mjs` (12 and 16 players in every tournament type), `tests/switch-online-play.spec.ts`.

31. **Table talk (owner decisions, 14 Nov 2026).** The 18 clean reactions are cheeky pub banter: teasing and mock outrage, never innuendo or drink. (15 Nov 2026: 37 optional "spicy" sayings with every swear word masked were added, off by default per phone, so the app is no longer 4+; see docs/store/LISTING.md.) Ten sayings (Read 'em and weep, Is that all you've got?, Sweating yet?, Oh, you absolute sausage, Pick up, sunshine, Who taught you to play?, Lucky so-and-so, Not today, sweetheart, Revenge is coming, Behave!) and eight of our own card-character stickers in place of emoji (Smug King "Too easy", Salty shaker, Card shark, Sweating ace "Gulp", Mic drop, Facepalm jack, Crying joker "Boo hoo", Seven on fire). You can react against the computer too, and the computers banter back in character: after a penalty, a spotted Last Card, someone reaching Last Card, a win, or a reply to you, at most one remark every few seconds. Computers at an online table do not banter. Every reaction is logged with its game and move for stats (Me > Table talk), and every game can be replayed at speed from History with the running stats and the talk. Code: `src/switch/reactions.ts`, `src/switch/gameReplay.ts`, `src/screens/tableTalk.tsx`, `src/screens/ReplayScreen.tsx`; tests: `tests/switch-table-talk.spec.ts`.

33. **The dealer's turned-up card (owner decision, 15 Nov 2026).** Now and then, about one deal in five (`POWER_START_CHANCE`), the dealer turns the starter honestly instead of tucking a power card back, so a power card opens roughly one game in eleven. The card counts as the dealer's own play: a 7 means the dealer plays again; a jack reverses play (with two players the dealer plays again); a 2, an 8 or a black king puts the penalty on the next player (pick up 2, miss a turn, pick up 5), who may counter as usual; a red king does nothing; an ace is not played by anyone, so play starts in the ace's own suit. The table says what happened. Other deals still turn an ordinary card. Applies to every game against the computer, online tables and the guided game. Code: `openingEffect`/`openingNote` in `src/switch/table.ts`, `createInitialGame` in `src/switch/rules.ts`; tests: `tests/switch-opening.spec.ts`.

34. **An ace played as the last card keeps its own suit (owner decision, 15 Nov 2026; refines rulings 2 and 24).** An ace that empties the player's hand, alone or as a set of aces, does not let them choose a suit: the ace stays in the suit it was printed in, and the next player has to match that suit (or play an ace). This matters in play-on games (Knock Out, Group Play, play-offs), where the player goes through to the next round but the game carries on for everyone else. Any other ace play still chooses the suit. Engine: `legalActionsFor`, `isLegalPlayFor`, `applyAction` in `src/switch/rules.ts`, `applyTableAction` in `src/switch/table.ts`, rebuilt into `api/_engine.js`; screens skip the suit picker when the ace is the last card.

35. **Computers sometimes forget Last Card too (owner request, 15 Nov 2026; adds to ruling 20).** Against the computer, when a computer goes down to one card there is a small chance it forgets to say "Last Card": 15% on Easy, 8% on Medium, 3% on Hard. It is caught straight after its move: the red HAHA banner names it and it picks up two cards. Screen: `src/screens/TableGame.tsx` (`forgetChance`). Computers at online tables are unchanged. Automated test browsers never forget unless a test sets `switchForgetLastCard` to a chance.

36. **Last One Out, and choosing your computers (owner requests, 16 Nov 2026; adds to rulings 11, 25 and 30).** A new way to play, "Last One Out" (mode id `lastout`), against the computer and at an online table of friends. Everyone plays the same game together and it is played on until one player is left holding cards, who drops out. The rest are dealt in again: five become four, four become three, three become two, and the last two play a 1 v 1 final. There are no leagues and no points: places are the order of dropping out, the last one standing wins. It needs at least 3 players (up to 8 against the computer, up to 16 online, split across tables as in ruling 32). It is the same game that ruling 11 called "Knock Out with friends"; online it replaces Knock Out in the picker (the server still accepts `knockout` for older tables, with the same behaviour), and Knock Out as a way to play is dropped everywhere (owner decision, 16 Nov 2026): it was almost the same as the Switch Cup, the head-to-head rounds of ruling 25, which stays. Games already saved as Knock Out still open and count; the scorekeeper's Knock Out night format is unchanged. Last One Out is listed first and counts as a Knock Out win for badges and trophies. Every way to play now has a one-line description in the picker and a fuller "how it plays, and how it scores" panel for the one chosen (`how` and `scoring` in `src/switch/series.ts`).

    You may pick which computers you face from John, Paul, George, Ringo, Ziggy, Marley and Bowie, or leave it to chance ("Pick at random"). Picks fill seats first; any other seat is drawn at random from the rest, and a table with no picks is a surprise (`chooseComputers`). At an online table the host's picks fill the empty seats when the table starts (a guest already using a name keeps it), up to all but two seats; the server stores only known names (`cleanComputers` in `api/_room.js`). Tests: `tests/switch-game-types.spec.ts`, `tests/room.test.mjs`.

37. **Last One Out replaces Knock Out in the scorekeeper, with rounds to win (owner request, 16 Nov 2026; adds to rulings 11 and 36).** The scorekeeper's Knock Out night is now called Last One Out everywhere, and so is its saved-night label. A round is played as before: whoever finishes last is out (LOST) until one player is left, and that player takes a **round win**. You choose how many round wins take the night: a one-off game (1), or first to 2, 3 and more (up to 9 on the scorekeeper). After a round that does not settle it, everyone is back in and "Start round N" deals the next one; undo steps back through eliminations and into the previous round. Five players play 5, 4, 3, 2 and the last one standing wins the round. The same choice (one-off or first to 2 to 5 round wins) is on the Play lobby against the computer and on a table of friends; it is stored as `roundsToWin` on the series setup, defaults to one round, and the standings show each player's round wins (`roundWins` in `src/switch/series.ts`, `api/_room.js`). Computer Last One Out stats are unchanged: a series is one game for records and badges. Code: `src/switch/knockout.ts`, `src/screens/Scorekeeper.tsx`, `src/switch/series.ts`. Tests: `tests/switch-scorekeeper.spec.ts`, `tests/switch-game-types.spec.ts`, `tests/room.test.mjs`.

38. **One deck only, so at most six at a table (owner decision, 18 Nov 2026; replaces ruling 21 and changes rulings 22, 29, 30 and 32).** Switch is only ever played with one 52-card deck, and there must always be a pile to pick up from. Six players take 42 cards and the starter makes 43, which leaves nine to pick up from; a seventh player would leave only two. So one game of Switch has 2 to 6 players everywhere: against the computer you play 1 to 5 computers (you pick them from John, Paul, George, Ringo, Ziggy, Marley and Bowie, or leave it to chance), and at an online table a single game, Games, Darts scoring and Dominoes scoring are for up to 6. Online tournaments still take up to 16 people, but every game is at a table of up to 6: Last One Out and the play-offs split onto the fewest tables of up to 6 (16 make three tables of 6, 5 and 5), and Group Play is for 6 to 12 so its two leagues are never more than 6 (12 make two leagues of 6). A king pile-up is therefore at most 10 cards (two black kings), as the engine already counts it. Games of 7 or 8 that were started before this ruling can still be finished and still count in every stat; no new one can be dealt. The two badges for a table of 8 are now for a full table of 6, and games at 7 or 8 still earn them. Code: `MAX_SEATS` in `src/switch/table.ts`, `MAX_COMPUTERS`, `TABLE_MAX` and `ONLINE_MAX_PLAYERS` in `src/switch/series.ts`, rebuilt into `api/_engine.js`.
